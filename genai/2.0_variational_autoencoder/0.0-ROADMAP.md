# Variational Autoencoder Learning Roadmap

This directory implements the eight VAE variant units retained by the
companion reading guide. The scripts are algorithm lessons, not a production
training framework: each entry keeps its direct baseline, objective,
probability interface, gradient boundaries, and sampling
path visible. Shared network blocks and evaluation utilities live in
`dl_utils/vae/`.

The file groups encode the recommended learning order. They do not imply that
beta-VAE, cVAE, IWAE, or later branches accumulate all earlier changes.

## 1. Completed Baseline

| Lesson | Role |
|---|---|
| `0.1_autoencoder.py` | Deterministic reconstruction baseline |
| `1.0_vae.py` | Standard diagonal-Gaussian VAE and reparameterization |
| `1.1_vae_evaluation.py` | Posterior reconstruction versus prior generation |

The introductory VAE, beta-VAE, cVAE, HVAE/Ladder, VQ-VAE/FSQ, and VQGAN
lessons use 256x256 faces from glasses-256. IWAE uses 32x32 MNIST images.
The beta-VAE lesson deliberately shares the standard VAE model and training
setup, changing only the KL weight.

Standard VAE and beta-VAE reuse `ImageEncoder` and `ImageDecoder` from
`dl_utils/vae/vae_common.py`: six convolutional down/up stages with GroupNorm
and SiLU. Their default `z_dim=100`, `hidden_channels=256` model has 6.812 M
parameters, with a linear posterior head over the flattened 4x4 features.
The earlier 63.33 M weights are incompatible; rerun 1.0 and 2.0 before their
evaluations. New checkpoints record the `shared_rgb` backbone and channel width.

## 2. Step 1: Beta-VAE

| Lesson | Direct baseline | Visible increment | Focused evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `2.0_beta_vae.py` | `1.0_vae.py` | Scale the complete per-sample KL by beta while sharing data, model, optimizer, and training loop | A directly inspectable `D + beta * KL` objective |
| `2.1_beta_vae_evaluation.py` | Matched standard-VAE and beta-VAE checkpoints | Compare both models on the same images and prior samples | Rate-distortion, per-dimension KL, active units, reconstruction, and collapse diagnostics |

These capacity diagnostics do not establish semantic disentanglement because
the face dataset has no independent ground-truth generative factors.

After training, the shared VAE/beta-VAE trainer saves `vae_metrics.csv` in
`output/vae/vae/` and `beta_vae_metrics.csv` in `output/vae/beta_vae/`, alongside
each model's `loss_curves.png`. Both CSV files contain `epoch`, `total`,
`reconstruction`, and unweighted `kl`; beta is already applied in `total`.

## 3. Step 2: Conditional VAE

| Lesson | Direct baseline | Visible increment | Focused evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `3.0_conditional_vae.py` | Standard VAE concept; same face data as cGAN | Condition the posterior, learned Gaussian prior, and decoder | Summed RGB MSE, conditional KL, and fixed-noise class samples |
| `3.1_conditional_vae_evaluation.py` | Saved glasses-256 cVAE checkpoint | Inspect conditional generation and reconstruction without training an auxiliary model | Paired-noise class samples, original/mean-reconstruction rows, and training-set MSE/KL diagnostics |

Both scripts read `data/glasses-256` directly as 256x256 RGB images in [0, 1],
without creating a new cache. The class indices match the conditional GAN:
`G=0` (with glasses) and `NoG=1` (without glasses). The cGAN instead normalizes
its inputs to [-1, 1]; both models' saved sample images are displayed in [0, 1].
The cVAE uses a 128-dimensional latent, a 32-dimensional class embedding, and
six convolutional down/up stages. Encoder channels are
`32 -> 64 -> 128 -> 256 -> 256 -> 256`, reducing 256x256 to 4x4; the decoder
mirrors these widths back to RGB. The posterior uses a 512-unit hidden layer
before its 128-dimensional mean and log-variance outputs. The image backbone
is shared with standard VAE/beta-VAE; the conditional networks add capacity.
The default model contains approximately 8.558 million parameters.

The objective is summed-pixel MSE plus `KL[q(z | x, c) || p(z | c)]`, using
the same fixed Gaussian observation scale as the introductory face VAE.
The target image is available only to the recognition posterior. Generation
transforms Gaussian base noise through the learned conditional prior, then
decodes with the requested label. Sample rows are G then NoG, with shared base
noise in corresponding columns; identity preservation is not guaranteed.

Training defaults to 80 epochs, batch size 16, and all 4,500 images, matching
the cGAN's data scope. Adam uses betas `(0.9, 0.999)` and an initial learning
rate of `2e-4`, with cosine decay toward `2e-5`. The per-epoch learning rate is
saved alongside loss metrics; scheduler settings are recorded in the checkpoint.
Evaluation therefore reports training-set diagnostics,
not held-out performance. Matching the dataset and resolution supports visual
comparison; the different architectures, objectives, and compute budgets do
not constitute a controlled quality benchmark.

The trainer saves fixed-noise `training/epoch_*.png` after epoch 1, every
`SAMPLE_EVERY` epochs, and the final epoch. It then saves `cvae_metrics.csv`,
metric curves, final class samples, and `conditional_vae.pth` directly in
`output/vae/conditional_vae/`. The checkpoint records model configuration,
class order, image format, objective, and training hyperparameters. The
evaluation entry loads this checkpoint and saves `conditional_samples.png`,
`real_reconstruction.png`
(original/reconstruction rows for G, then NoG), `metric_summary.png`, and
`metrics.json` under `evaluation/`. Former MNIST checkpoints and the earlier
100-dimensional face architecture need retraining for this configuration.

A local teaching run compared the 80-epoch cosine recipe with a 120-epoch
constant-`1e-4` baseline on an RTX 4070 Ti. Both produced recognizable faces
and visible glasses conditioning. The selected recipe took about 7m16s
versus 10m52s, with training-set evaluation losses of 583.03 versus 557.34
(summed MSE plus conditional KL). It trades a modest reconstruction gap for
one-third fewer updates; it is not a claim of better held-out performance.
Independent prior samples still show occasional glasses traces in the NoG
class and soft fine details. Local source snapshots, checkpoints, metrics,
sample grids, and the comparison report are under `output/vae/cvae_experiments/`.

Both entries reuse `dl_utils.data.loading.make_device_aware_loader` and
`dl_utils.training.checkpoints` for data loading and model serialization.
`dl_utils.vae.conditional_vae` owns the model, cache metadata contract, loss,
and evaluation figures. Its sampling path reuses the fixed class-noise grid
and bounded inference helpers in `dl_utils.inference`, the shared Gaussian
reparameterization in `vae_common`, and `dl_utils.plot.images` for labeled
image rows. The renderer's [-1, 1] convention is adapted only at display time;
model inputs and outputs remain in [0, 1]. Optimization order, hyperparameters,
sampling cadence, and output writes remain explicit in the lesson entries.

## 4. Step 3: Importance-Weighted Autoencoder

| Lesson | Direct baseline | Visible increment | Focused evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `4.0_iwae.py` | Standard VAE | Replace the one-sample ELBO with a stable per-example K-particle bound | K=1, K=4, K=8, K=16, and K=32, ESS/K, and equal training epochs |
| `4.1_iwae_evaluation.py` | IWAE runs with different K | Re-evaluate every run with the same large-K estimator | Bound uncertainty, rate, active units, reconstruction, and prior samples |

K changes the objective estimator and compute budget, not the posterior or
decoder family.

## 5. Steps 4-5: Hierarchical and Ladder VAEs

| Step and lesson | Direct baseline | Visible increment | Focused evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 · `5.0_hierarchical_vae.py` | Standard VAE | Add a second latent, `p(z1 | z2)`, two KL terms, and top-down sampling | Per-layer KL, generation order, and single-layer replacement |
| 5 · `5.1_ladder_vae.py` | Two-level HVAE | Fuse bottom-up evidence with the matching top-down prior by precision | Matched generator/capacity comparison and per-layer utilization |
| `5.2_hierarchical_vae_evaluation.py` | Both retained hierarchy models | Intervene on one stochastic layer at a time | Per-layer KL, single-layer replacement distortion increases, and controlled resampling grids |

The two scripts share the same generative hierarchy so the Ladder comparison
isolates its inference-network increment. Both read all 4,500 glasses-256
images directly as 256x256 RGB and ignore class labels. They reuse the CVAE
image backbone and optimize summed RGB MSE plus two KL terms, with matching
warm-up, group-wise free bits, and training settings. Fixed noise at both
levels makes sample grids comparable across epochs. Evaluation uses the same
training images and reports layer diagnostics, not held-out generalization.
The sampled posterior reconstruction is the baseline for each replacement.
The four-row reconstruction grid instead follows distribution means, using
z2=0 for top replacement. Two further grids fix the top latent while resampling
the lower prior, or fix image evidence while resampling the top posterior;
these observations do not establish a semantic global/local division.
Matching the VAE/CVAE dataset improves visual comparison; their different
capacities and training budgets still prevent a controlled model ranking.
Old 32x32 FactorShapes checkpoints must be regenerated with lessons 5.0/5.1.

## 6. Steps 6-7: VQ-VAE and FSQ

| Step and lesson | Direct baseline | Visible increment | Focused evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 · `6.0_vq_vae.py` | Autoencoder | Learned vector quantization, STE, EMA codebook updates, commitment loss, and 16x spatial compression | Reconstruction, token usage, and unconditional PixelCNN generation |
| 7 · `6.1_fsq.py` | Matched VQ-VAE interface | Replace learned lookup and commitment loss with fixed scalar levels | Matched reconstruction, token utilization, prior NLL, and generation |
| `6.2_discrete_tokenizer_evaluation.py` | Both tokenizers and the standard Gaussian VAE | Add two visual comparisons to the existing discrete-model diagnostics | Same-image reconstruction and independent unconditional samples |

Both lessons use the same 4,500 glasses-256 faces as `1.0_vae.py`, without
class conditioning, cropping, or random flips. The prepared images are read at
256x256; the discrete models normalize to [-1, 1], while the Gaussian VAE uses
[0, 1]. All saved images use the same display range. This small dataset is a
teaching starting point for visual comparison, not evidence of generalization.
Switching only the discrete models to a larger face dataset would change the
comparison task; a larger-data comparison also requires retraining the VAE.

Tokenizer training and prior training remain separate stages. Four downsampling
steps map 256x256 images to 16x16 token grids with 512 possible codes: learned
VQ uses 512 vectors, while FSQ uses levels `(8, 8, 8)`. Both have 256 tokens and
a nominal fixed-length rate of 2,304 bits per image. Their latent channel counts
and quantizer objectives differ by design. FSQ uses centered integer codes,
an even-level offset, and straight-through rounding; its latent MSE is not on
the same coordinate scale as VQ latent MSE.

VQ-VAE and VQGAN default to `EMA_DECAY=0.99` and `EMA_EPSILON=1e-5`.
For each training batch, the quantizer exponentially averages each code's
assignment count and the sum of its assigned encoder vectors. It divides the
vector sums by Laplace-smoothed counts to update the codebook without optimizer
gradients. Both statistics start at zero; never-assigned codes retain their
initial vectors. Outputs and commitment targets use the codebook from before
that batch's update. EMA keeps the straight-through encoder gradient and
commitment loss, while `quantization_mse` reports the encoder-to-code distance.
Evaluation, frozen-token caching, and prior training leave the codebook and EMA
statistics unchanged. This is codebook learning, separate from a moving average
of whole-model weights used for inference.

Both entries default to batch size 16 and 100 epochs for each stage. The
tokenizers use matching image backbones and the priors share the same
64-channel, 16-layer gated PixelCNN. Separate vertical and horizontal streams
cover the complete 16x16 causal past. Neither stage uses the G/NoG folder
labels. Frozen tokenizer outputs are cached once for prior training.

PixelCNN generation reuses intermediate activations during the raster scan.
The vertical stream is computed once per row; the horizontal stream retains
only the previous positions needed by each convolution. CUDA sampling captures
and replays a one-token CUDA Graph to reduce Python and kernel-launch overhead;
CPU sampling uses the same caches without a graph. Caches are local to each
sampling call and never enter a checkpoint. The 6.0/6.1 previews and 6.2 generation
use this path automatically, including with existing `pixelcnn_prior.pth` weights.
No tokenizer or prior retraining is required for the acceleration.

`PixelCNNPrior.sample(..., cached=False)` runs the original full-grid reference.
`cuda_graph=False` uses eager cached sampling, which can be useful for debugging
or small CUDA grids where graph setup is not worthwhile. Training and NLL
calculation still use the unchanged parallel full-grid forward pass. The cached
and reference paths evaluate the same categorical conditionals within
floating-point tolerance. Different operation shapes can change rounding, so
identical seeds need not produce identical sampled grids across paths.
This optimization follows the hidden-state caching principle in
[Fast Generation for Convolutional Autoregressive Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06001);
it retains the existing gated PixelCNN architecture and checkpoint format.

A seeded 256-image subset of the training set monitors each epoch and selects
the tokenizer by MSE and its prior by NLL. The shared checkpoint helper keeps
its existing `validation` / `val_*` fields; their protocol explicitly identifies
these observations as training-set diagnostics. Epoch sample grids contain
fixed reconstructions and 18 unconditional prior samples.

Run `1.0_vae.py`, `6.0_vq_vae.py`, and `6.1_fsq.py` before
`6.2_discrete_tokenizer_evaluation.py`. The evaluation defaults to a seeded
256-image training subset and retains the existing VQ/FSQ MSE, PSNR, token-use,
rate, prior-NLL diagnostics, and 64-image sample grids. It adds only two figures
under `output/vae/evaluation/discrete_tokenizer/`:

- `vae_vq_vae_fsq_reconstructions.png`: the same eight original images, VAE
  posterior-mean reconstructions, VQ-VAE reconstructions, and FSQ reconstructions.
- `vae_vq_vae_fsq_samples.png`: eight independent prior samples from each model;
  corresponding columns do not imply matching identities.

The Gaussian checkpoint participates only in these visual comparisons; its
existing evaluation remains in `1.1_vae_evaluation.py`. No FID/KID or additional
distributional metrics are introduced. Prior token cross-entropies exclude model
weights and entropy-coder overhead. The different Gaussian/discrete representation
budgets, priors, and objectives prevent attributing every visual difference to
quantization alone.

## 7. Step 8: VQGAN

| Lesson | Direct baseline | Visible increment | Focused evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `7.0_vqgan.py` | VQ-VAE plus completed GAN training skills | Train a 16x-compressed 256x256 tokenizer with frozen learned LPIPS, delayed PatchGAN, adaptive adversarial weighting, and a class-conditional frozen-token Transformer prior | Two-optimizer isolation, reconstruction, token usage, and prior generation |
| `7.1_vqgan_evaluation.py` | Completed VQGAN stage artifacts | Reload the default tokenizer, discriminator, and prior on glasses-256 training images | Paired fidelity, token rate, prior NLL, PatchGAN diagnostics, and G/NoG conditional sample grids |

Training uses learned LPIPS v0.1 with a frozen VGG trunk. After a delay,
[the shared adaptive weight](../../dl_utils/vae/perceptual_autoencoder.py)
balances reconstruction and adversarial gradient norms at the decoder's
last layer; the recorded adversarial scale exposes that balance. Generator
and discriminator updates remain explicit in the lesson. Transformer layers
are independently initialized after constructing the encoder stack.
The shared quantizer uses the same configurable EMA update as VQ-VAE, once
per autoencoder batch; the discriminator step reuses detached reconstructions
and does not update the codebook.

VQGAN uses the same 4,500 glasses-256 training images and [-1, 1]
normalization as VQ-VAE/FSQ, without cropping or random flips. The tokenizer
reconstructs without labels; its Transformer prior conditions generation on
G=0 (with glasses) and NoG=1 (without glasses). Four downsampling steps
compress 256x256 images to a 16x16 grid;
the decoder reconstructs 256x256 RGB images. Its 512-code vocabulary gives
256 tokens and 2,304 fixed-length bits per image. The Transformer models all
256 tokens causally. VQGAN uses batch size 16 and 30 epochs per stage.
Different conditioning, backbones, objectives, priors, and training budgets prevent
a controlled quality comparison with the VQ-VAE/FSQ lessons.

A seeded 1,024-image training subset selects the tokenizer by L1 plus weighted
LPIPS and its prior by NLL. The shared validation / val_* checkpoint fields
record this training-set monitoring protocol. Evaluation defaults to all
4,500 training images; MAX_EXAMPLES records a seeded subset for shorter checks.
The resulting metrics are training-set diagnostics. Saved reconstructions and
64 conditional samples use 256x256 images; sample columns alternate G and
NoG, and metrics.json records each generated image's class ID. Prior NLL
uses the image labels and excludes their coding cost. The evaluation script
is a companion, not a ninth retained model.

## 8. Running the Teaching Sequence

Scripts 3.0-7.1 run without command-line arguments. Edit their top-level
constants to explore hyperparameters. The implementations emphasize the
algorithm and do not aim to reproduce paper results exactly.

- Run each training entry before its evaluation: 3.0 then 3.1; 4.0 then 4.1;
  both 5.0 and 5.1 before 5.2; 1.0, 6.0, and 6.1 before 6.2; 7.0 before 7.1.
- VQ-VAE, FSQ, and VQGAN train the tokenizer first, select weights, freeze it,
  and then train its prior. VQ-VAE/FSQ select by training-subset MSE and prior
  NLL; VQGAN uses training-subset L1 plus weighted LPIPS and prior NLL.
- The three discrete-tokenizer entries use `RESUME=True` to continue matching
  epoch-boundary checkpoints automatically. Set `RESUME=False` for a fresh
  recipe, or `TRAIN_TOKENIZER=False` to load selected tokenizer weights and run
  only the prior. After changing the prior recipe or selected tokenizer,
  use prior-only mode with `RESUME=False` to fit the prior again.
- VQ checkpoints record the EMA settings in `model_config` and include both
  EMA buffers in every model state. Resume requires matching settings.
- Each discrete stage writes `tokenizer/` or `prior/`: `latest.pth` contains
  model, optimizer, RNG, history, and selection state; `best.pth` and `last.pth`
  contain evaluated model weights. Recovery is saved before validation and
  plotting, and interrupted validation is replayed without retraining that
  epoch. At most the incomplete training epoch must be repeated.
- Selected weights are exported under the familiar root filenames. Priors and
  token caches record the exact tokenizer snapshot ID. Loaders validate this
  pairing together with image preprocessing, label semantics, vocabulary, and
  Transformer sequence length. All three lessons, shared model defaults, and
  weight loaders use `TOKENIZER_DOWNSAMPLE_STEPS=4`. Weight loaders require
  the selected lesson's configuration, and evaluation derives grids from the
  loaded tokenizer. Token caches are
  rebuilt when the selected tokenizer changes.
- Other VAE lessons retain their existing final-weight and output-reset policies.
  Re-running a discrete evaluation clears only its evaluation directory.
- From cVAE onward, training saves `<prefix>_metrics.csv` and
  `<prefix>_metrics_01.png` (plus additional pages) together in each run's
  output root, alongside its checkpoint. This includes each IWAE `k*` run,
  each hierarchy `baseline` run, and both the `tokenizer` and `prior` metric
  sets for VQ-VAE, FSQ, and VQGAN. Epoch sample images are written
  synchronously to `training/` at each configured sampling epoch, before
  the next epoch starts. Final model weights and aggregate metrics are
  saved when their training stage finishes. Discrete stages additionally persist
  their per-epoch history in `<stage>/metrics.csv` and keep validation curves.
- KL warm-up, free bits, straight-through gradients, causal masks, and
  memory-bounded IWAE evaluation remain visible where the algorithm needs them.
- LPIPS uses pretrained weights. Discrete-tokenizer evaluations report
  reconstruction errors, token statistics, prior likelihood, and sample grids;
  VQGAN also reports LPIPS and PatchGAN diagnostics.
- The route ends after VQGAN and hands off to pixel-space DDPM. KL
  autoencoders and Latent Diffusion remain in `../3.0_diffusion_model/`.
